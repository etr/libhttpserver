4. [ ] **performance-reviewer** | `src/httpserver/detail/forms_multipart.hpp:505` | algorithmic-complexity
   pump_headers() calls pending_.erase(0, nl + 1) once per header line inside its loop, and each front-erase memmoves the entire remaining pending_ buffer. Under the streaming path (read_multipart feeds <=512 bytes, so pending_ stays <= feed + holdback) this is bounded and harmless, but under a single large feed -- exactly what the public installed one-shot forms::decode_multipart does via decoder.feed(body) -- pending_ holds the whole body while each of up to ~max_part_header_bytes/3 stored lines (a minimal stored line is one char + CRLF; the colon check happens later in record_header, so even junk lines count) shifts the full remaining tail. Worst case is O(lines x remaining) = ~cap^2/6 byte-moves: with the default 64 KiB max_total_bytes an adversarial body (short-line header blocks padded before a bulk tail) forces on the order of hundreds of MiB of memmove (~10,000x amplification of the input), and the amplification factor grows linearly with a caller-raised max_total_bytes (the e2e disk route already uses 16 MiB; an app one-shotting at that cap reaches tens of GiB of memmove per request). This is bounded by caps and NOT reachable through make_multipart_route/read_multipart (the server hot path), which is why it is major rather than critical.
   *Recommendation:* Consume header lines with a cursor instead of erasing per line, mirroring pump_body's from-offset technique: advance a consumed offset past each line (copying the line into lines_ as today), and perform one pending_.erase(0, consumed) when pump_headers exits (need_more, failure, or blank-line transition to begin_part). That makes the per-feed cost O(pending_) once per feed for every feed size, removing the quadratic for one-shot callers with no behavior change (the every_split matrix in test/unit/multipart_decoder_test.cpp is the regression net).
